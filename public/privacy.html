Privacy Policy

Effective November 14, 2026; updated November 19, 2026. Applies to the Visitor Recognizer web app at ring-visitor-recognizer.onrender.com, operated by Mussa Drammeh, an individual developer publishing as Aoi Corp (“we”). Visitor Recognizer works with Ring cameras through the Ring Appstore and is not made or endorsed by Ring.

Summary

  • We process your Ring snapshots on our own server, hosted by Render in the United States. Nothing runs on your phone or camera.
  • Facial recognition is optional, off by default, and only ever recognizes people you enroll. Everyone else is “Unknown” and no face data is kept about them.
  • We never sell your data, never use it for advertising, and never share or compare it across customers.
  • Arrivals and snapshots are deleted after 30 days. You can delete anything, or everything, at any time.

Biometric information (facial recognition)

What: a face template is a list of numbers describing facial geometry, created by a face-recognition model that runs on our server. It is biometric information under laws such as the Illinois Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA), the Texas Capture or Use of Biometric Identifier Act, the Washington biometric identifier law, and the California Consumer Privacy Act.

When: only after you turn on facial recognition by ticking an unchecked consent box and confirming. Consent to facial recognition is separate from creating an account and from connecting Ring.

Whose: only people you enroll yourself, one at a time, after confirming each of them agreed to be enrolled. We never enroll anyone automatically, never use faces from any other source, and have no database of faces of public figures or anyone else. Faces of people you have not enrolled are compared with your enrollments only to decide they are “Unknown”; their templates are discarded immediately and never stored.

Purpose: only to show you which enrolled person arrived. We do not use biometric data for marketing, advertising, analytics or model training, and we do not sell, lease, trade or otherwise profit from it.

Where and how it's protected: templates are stored only in our database (Neon, AWS us-east-2, United States), encrypted with AES-256-GCM, transmitted only over TLS, and are only ever compared with snapshots from the cameras you share from your one connected Ring account (each Visitor Recognizer account can connect only one Ring account; if that Ring account has cameras at more than one address, recognition covers all the cameras you shared). No other customer, and no other customer's cameras, can match against your enrollments.

Retention and destruction: templates are permanently deleted when you delete the person, when you turn off facial recognition, when you delete your data or account, or automatically one year after your last sign-in — whichever comes first.

Disclosure: we do not disclose biometric data to anyone, except where required by law or valid legal process.

State-law notice: facial recognition is not offered for cameras that Ring reports in Illinois, Texas or Washington, where the law requires the enrolled person's own written release. Arrival logging still works there. Wherever you are, you are responsible for getting the permission of each person you enroll and for complying with laws that apply to you.

How data is processed

Ring sends us an event when a shared doorbell is pressed or a shared camera detects motion. Our server then requests the snapshot for that moment from Ring's official Partner API, blacks out your privacy zones, and stores the result encrypted. If facial recognition is on and you have enrolled people, the server compares faces in the snapshot with your enrollments. All of this happens on the Visitor Recognizer server; no third-party face service is used.

Sharing

  • Render (render.com) hosts our server in the United States. It does not use customer data for its own purposes.
  • Neon (neon.com) hosts our Postgres database on AWS in the United States (us-east-2). Snapshots, templates and tokens are stored there encrypted with our key, and Neon does not use them for its own purposes.
  • Ring provides the events and snapshots you authorize. We send Ring your masked Visitor Recognizer email (for example a***e@example.com) when you connect, so Ring can show which account is linked.

We share nothing else, with no one else. We do not use your snapshots, videos or any other Ring data to train AI or machine learning models, and no person views your snapshots or face templates. No data is sold. When you delete data, it is deleted from our live database immediately. We make no separate copies or backups of customer content. Our database provider keeps a rolling change history of up to 6 hours for recovery; deleted data leaves it within 6 hours, and it holds only data encrypted with our key.

Your choices and rights

  • Revoke facial-recognition consent any time in the app (Facial recognition → Turn off). All templates are deleted immediately and permanently.
  • Delete an enrolled person any time; it is immediate and permanent, and confirmed in the app.
  • Delete any arrival, or delete all my data, or delete my account in Privacy and your data. Each asks you to confirm and shows a confirmation when done. Deletion requests are recorded in a deletion log.
  • Download your data as a file in the app.
  • Disconnect Ring in the Ring app (Ring Appstore → My Apps → Visitor Recognizer → Remove). We delete your Ring tokens as soon as Ring notifies us.
  • If an enrolled person asks you to remove them, delete them in the app. They can also contact us at mussadrammeh@protonmail.com.
  • California and other US residents may request access, correction or deletion by email; we respond within 30 days and do not discriminate for exercising rights.

Security

TLS in transit; AES-256-GCM at rest for tokens, emails, templates, snapshots and records; scrypt password hashing; HttpOnly secure session cookies with CSRF protection and sign-in rate limiting; per-account access checks on every request; an activity log you can view. See Security.

Children

Visitor Recognizer is not directed to children and accounts are for adults (18+). Do not enroll a child unless you are their parent or guardian.

Changes and contact

We will post changes here and update the effective date; material changes to how biometric data is used will require new consent in the app. Questions: mussadrammeh@protonmail.com.
